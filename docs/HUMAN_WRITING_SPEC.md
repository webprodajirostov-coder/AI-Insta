# Human Writing Spec

This document is the shared content-quality standard for natural, author-specific Russian and English writing.

## Principle
Do not optimize for beating a detector. Optimize for writing that sounds like a real, specific author: concrete, situated, varied, and appropriate to the audience and platform.

No single feature proves AI authorship. Review clusters of signals.

## Strong shared signals
- Uniform sentence rhythm / low sentence-length variance.
- Repeated sentence openings and syntactic templates.
- Formulaic paragraph architecture.
- Repeated discourse markers and transitions.
- Repeated wording or semantic recycling.
- Predictable conclusions that merely restate the introduction.
- Generic hedging: important to note / it is worth noting / важно отметить / следует отметить.
- Polished vagueness: fluent language without concrete commitments.
- Excessive explicitness: every logical relation is spelled out.
- Over-organization: headings/lists/symmetry beyond what the format needs.
- Flat authorial voice.
- Vocabulary that is more formal, abstract, or corporate than the speaker would naturally use.
- Suspiciously uniform punctuation patterns.
- Style mismatch between sections.
- Confident claims without the specificity or caveats expected from a real expert.

## Russian
Watch especially for:
- abstract noun stacks and bureaucratic nominalization;
- excessive: «важно отметить», «следует отметить», «таким образом», «кроме того», «в свою очередь», «более того», «следовательно»;
- repetitive «это не просто X, а Y» and three-part rhetoric;
- repeated sentence starts;
- dash-heavy constructions used as a universal pause;
- uniformly long/high-register words in casual content;
- unnecessary foreign-language switching;
- semantic repetition disguised as synonyms.

Prefer concrete verbs, ordinary words, audience-specific situations, natural compression, and varied rhythm.

## English
Watch especially for:
- repeated: “It is important to…”, “It is worth noting…”, “Furthermore…”, “Moreover…”, “This highlights…”, “In conclusion…”;
- inflated vocabulary: “delve”, “underscore”, “tapestry”, “nuanced”, “multifaceted”, “robust”, “seamless”, “leverage”, when generic;
- abstract nouns: “landscape”, “journey”, “framework”, “ecosystem”, “paradigm” without topic-specific need;
- repeated balanced constructions: “not only X, but also Y”, “on the one hand… on the other hand…”;
- excessive nominalization;
- absence of contractions in genuinely casual speech;
- repetitive paragraph cadence.

## Do not fake humanity
Never add random typos, fake experiences, random slang, deliberately bad grammar, or mechanical sentence-length randomness.

## Human Voice QA
After drafting:
1. Mark repeated sentence openings.
2. Mark repeated connectors.
3. Mark duplicated ideas.
4. Identify generic or overly abstract sentences.
5. Check sentence-length and paragraph-size variation.
6. Check whether vocabulary matches the speaker.
7. Replace only the highest-value passages.
8. Preserve intentional repetition and real voice.

## Scoring
Score each 0–2: rhythm, repetition, discourse markers, structure, hedging, abstraction, syntax, punctuation, voice, specificity, conclusion predictability, vocabulary fit.
0–6: healthy. 7–12: selective edit. 13–17: strong AI-style risk. 18+: rewrite sections.

This is a writing-quality score, not an authorship detector.
